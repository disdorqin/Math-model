# MCM/ICM Excellent-Paper Library for a 1028-City Resource-Sharing Network Paper

Research date: 2026-11-22. All pages read 2026-11-22 via web index/full-text extraction (flagged "index" below); no live-browser interaction was needed because all sources are static PDFs/lists.

## Summary

**Scope as finally specified:** 2024–2026 MCM/ICM Outstanding (O) and Finalist (F) papers first, downloadable full-text PDFs prioritised; pre-2024 papers only as labelled **exceptions** where exceptionally on-topic.

**What was verified and catalogued: 51 entries.**
- **16 papers from 2024–2025 with working direct PDF URLs** (all Outstanding; 14 verified Grade A against COMAP or republications of COMAP per-problem lists, 2 Grade B archive-verified and labelled).
- **19 papers from 2026** whose O/F status is verified from COMAP's official 2026 results pages — but **no downloadable solution PDFs were located** in the public archives searched (beldore.club, ModelScope) as of 2026-11-22. This is stated per entry, as instructed.
- **6 Finalist-award papers from 2024** verified from the 2024 award list — PDFs likewise not located (stated per entry).
- **10 pre-2024 exceptions**, all with PDF URLs, including the three most on-topic papers in the whole library (2019 Louvre network flow, 2021 wildfire facility+MST design, 2023 SDG network perturbation).

**Deep reads (Tier 1):** 7 papers analysed in full for method / figures / writing — 2024 MCM B 2425792, 2024 ICM D 2429211, 2025 ICM D 2504188, 2024 ICM D 2417831, plus exceptions 2019 ICM D 1924801, 2021 MCM B 2116496, 2023 ICM D 2304962 (with 2020 ICM D 2022868 and 2022 MCM B 2200084 analysed at the same depth in the exceptions section).

**Headline conclusion for the target paper:** the winning pattern for network problems is *cluster/partition → constrained tree or flow optimisation → perturbation-based robustness*, presented with a problem-by-problem Summary that reports a headline number for every sub-problem, a network diagram before the equations, and a named sensitivity/robustness section. Full synthesis, figure plan, and Summary template in §6.

**Verification grades used throughout:** **A** = team number appears in a COMAP official results document or a republication of COMAP's per-problem Outstanding/Finalist list. **B** = the ModelScope "Math-Modeling-BAO" archive files the PDF under an `/outstanding/` folder and COMAP confirms that problem/year had an Outstanding set, but no accessible list ties the specific team number. Grade B is never silently upgraded.

### Skim takeaways, Catalogue A (method / figures / writing, targeted at the 1028-city paper)

- **A2 (2407038):** ARIMA + ridge regression to fit continuous environmental fields before dynamics; search region rasterised to a grid with a Poisson probability distribution — the grid+probability treatment is a clean template for mapping distortion/feasibility over 1028 scattered cities. Sensitivity reported as an *insensitivity bound with numbers* ("probability change rate < 5.8% if time-interval change rate < 10%").
- **A3 (2411570):** Two-part ODE position model (disturbance + resistance) — structurally identical to "distortion accumulates along a path from two causes"; EWM-TOPSIS for equipment choice = a defensible way to choose *which* cities get strong links when several criteria compete.
- **A4 (2419984):** Grid + Monte Carlo location probabilities, then **ant-colony search path with Bayesian updating** — the Bayesian-update-after-each-observation loop is the exact shape of Problem 2's cross-correction: each additional source copy updates the posterior error. Headline: "43% probability of finding within 10 hours" — a probabilistic headline number, not just a cost.
- **A9 (2405424):** Builds **three competing model variants** on the same bases and compares them (ablation by model family) — replicate with weak-only / weak+strong / multi-source variants of the city network and compare in one table.
- **A12 (2509557):** System-dynamics model + L-BFGS-B welfare maximisation; multi-source data fused into dynamic parameters.
- **A14 (2502355):** ODE first, *then* a graph-theory network with populations as nodes and explicit stability scores per intervention (bats 29.29, bees 43.37, do-nothing 56.99) — shows how to attach a single comparable score to each candidate network intervention.
- **A15 (2517273):** Two-model architecture — a process model (BEME, Holling I–IV responses, 27 parameters tuned by differential evolution, +35% estimation accuracy) paired with an accounting model (SEAM: regenerative practice +48% ROI over 20 years once ecosystem services are monetised). **Direct analogue for strong links:** a 10,000× cost is only justifiable with a long-horizon/full-accounting model beside the network model. Caveat: the archive copy's Summary OCR is badly garbled; skim was limited to fragments + indexed snippets.
- **A16 (2504223):** Causal stack for policy questions (multi-period difference-in-differences *with parallel-trend and placebo tests*, survival analysis, SEM with fit test, VBGMM clustering); opens with global-distribution visualisation before modelling — map-first EDA.
- **A10 (2501687):** Monetises implicit costs (Contingent Valuation) to build **one** total utility, then multi-objective nonlinear programming + PSO; optimal values quoted precisely (peak cap 13,993 tourists; $92,310.46/day; 54.33:45.67 split); transfer test to Jiuzhaigou *changes* the policy conclusion — a transfer that changes an answer is stronger than one that confirms. Ranks its own recommendations ("the first policy is the most critical").
- **A11 (2503268):** **Best sensitivity template in the library:** Morris global screening (μ–σ diagram, names the two strongest parameters) *plus* local partial derivatives with values (792.18, 575.2 vs 0.017). Every sensitivity claim carries a number.

## 2. Tier 1 deep reads (2024–2026 papers + the network core)

### T1-A. 2024 ICM D, team 2429211, Harvey Mudd College — "Dynamic Dams Model" (Catalogue A5)
- **Verification:** Grade A — 2024 award list (MAA Award + COMAP Scholarship Award); COMAP's official 2024 Scholarship press release names team 2429211, Harvey Mudd, advisor Susan Martonosi; Harvey Mudd's own news confirms the team earned the Outstanding designation (one of four Outstanding winners on the problem, the only US one).
- **Model:** The Great Lakes are modelled as a **dynamic flow network**; two control algorithms use **linear programming** to compute optimal dam schedules and water levels; a "multigranular, human-centered" layer evaluates schedules at several stakeholder granularities.
- **Why it matters for the target paper:** this is the closest 2024–2026 analogue to Problem 1/2 thinking — a physical network abstracted to nodes/edges with capacities, an LP deciding flows, and a second layer translating LP output into stakeholder-readable consequences. Steal: (method) LP on a flow network with explicit capacity constraints ↔ LP/heuristic on the city tree with distortion budgets as the "capacity"; (figures) the network schematic carries the model definition; (writing) keywords line names the method ("Linear Program; Dynamic Flow Network") so a judge can classify the paper in five seconds.

### T1-B. 2025 ICM D, team 2504188 — "Revitalizing Transport: A Safer, Smarter Future for Baltimore's Network" (Catalogue A13, Grade B)
- **Model:** Three models. (1) **Bridge Collapse Impact Quantification** — the Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse treated as a network failure event (the single closest published analogue to Problem 3's city failures). (2) Transit Network Optimization: bus network built, station importance measured by **betweenness centrality and degree centrality**, then a nonlinear program maximises coverage subject to station-count/distance constraints, with Monte Carlo simulation to evaluate coverage over an irregular area — coverage rises **61.37% → 78.13%**. (3) Road Availability optimisation using Connectivity / Congestion / Mixing indexes and **Approximate Dynamic Programming** for path optimisation. Stakeholder ratings compared before/after; a software deliverable integrates the three models; closing sensitivity analysis.
- **Steal:** (method) centrality screening to find structurally weak nodes *before* optimising — run betweenness on the Problem-1 tree to pre-identify which cities' failure hurts most, then let Problem 3's random failures be evaluated against that ranking; coverage-as-objective with Monte Carlo evaluation ↔ "fraction of cities within distortion threshold" as the objective; (figures) network visualised in Task 1 before any optimisation (Figs. 6, 7, 9 carry the evaluation); (writing) each Task's summary paragraph ends with its before/after number.

### T1-C. 2024 ICM D, team 2417831, Tsinghua — "Harmony of the Great Lakes" (Catalogue A6)
- **Model:** A **genetic-algorithm** model balancing five objectives (coastal protection cost, hydropower, leisure satisfaction, shipping benefit, ecological impact); closes with a memo to the IJC leadership.
- **Steal:** (method) when the objective is genuinely multi-criteria and non-linear, a GA with an explicit five-term objective is an accepted O-level choice — the analogue is tuning the weak/strong mix where cost, distortion margin, and robustness trade off; (writing) the deliverable memo is part of the model output, not an appendix afterthought.

### T1-D. 2024 MCM B, team 2425792, Shanghai Jiao Tong Univ. — "Save the Submersible" (Catalogue A1)
- **Verification:** Grade A — 2024 award list: "2425792 Shanghai Jiao Tong University — SIAM Award" under Problem B.
- **Model:** Task 1: CFD via simplified Fossen 6-DOF equations + **Kalman filter predict–update to stop cumulative error growth** (the exact analogue of cumulative distortion along a path, and of Problem 2's cross-correction). Task 2: PCA over 14 equipment indicators → composite scores. Task 3: Kalman covariance + multivariate normal → 95% confidence 3D search volume; probability of find as a CDF over time. Task 4: extrapolation — re-run in the Caribbean (validation by transfer) + Voronoi diagram for multi-submersible cooperation.
- **Testing:** A combined Sensitivity *and* Error Analysis section with RMSE per parameter (seawater density 1.55 m, environment 2.10 m — high; propeller 1.12 m, mass 1.17 m — low): a tornado-style ranking in prose.
- **Summary:** Task verbs as sentence starters ("First, to locate. Then, to prepare. Next, to search. Furthermore, to extrapolate. Finally, sensitivity…"), each block 2–4 sentences of method + result, RMSE numbers included, keywords line.
- **Figures:** Three figures before any equation — background image, defects-and-consequences diagram, "Our Approach" flowchart.
- **Steal:** (method) recursive correction as the principled justification for fusion rules; uncertainty reported as a distribution/volume, not a point — report each city's distortion as an interval under parameter jitter; (figures) approach flowchart on p. 4; (writing) verb-led Summary paragraphs.

### Exceptions given Tier-1 treatment (pre-2024, exceptionally on-topic — see §4 for full entries)
- **2019 ICM D 1924801 (Louvre):** capacitated graph + max-flow/min-cut; edge deletion as the failure test (Richelieu closure: throughput 17.8 → 11.0 people/s, evacuation 24.34 → 39.39 min, +64%). The min-cut edge set, drawn in red on the network figure, is the model for "list all strong-link nodes": a *named, visualised critical set*, not a by-product table.
- **2021 MCM B 2116496 (wildfire drones):** cluster (k-means) → Kruskal MST per cluster → covering equation along edges (repeaters per channel) — the direct algorithmic skeleton for Problem 1 (cluster cities, constrained tree per cluster, relay/receiver count from a covering inequality). Solution maps print headline counts and budget in the map's corner.
- **2023 ICM D 2304962 (SDG network):** crisis modelled as a **perturbation matrix** applied to the network, with an anti-interference index; achieving/removing a node triggers re-ranking. This is the formal language for Problem 3: fail 20 cities = delete nodes, recompute, report the index.

## 6. Synthesis for the 1028-city paper

### (a) Common traits that distinguish O papers
1. **The Summary is a compressed version of the paper's logic**, organised problem-by-problem, with a headline number per problem and a closing robustness claim. Judges can reconstruct the whole solution from it.
2. **A named, staged pipeline** (brand the model; number the stages; the Summary mirrors the stages 1:1).
3. **A diagram before the equations** — framework flowchart or network schematic early (usually by p. 4).
4. **Validation is a designed experiment**, not a paragraph: perturbation/scenario tests (11/24) and sensitivity sections (20/24) with numbers; transfer tests when possible.
5. **Honesty as a feature**: weaknesses sections (21/24), rejected models declared, null results reported, worst-case bounds labelled as bounds.
6. **Results sections headed by findings** ("The Pyramid is NOT a Bottleneck"), not by methods.
7. **A deliverable** (memo/letter/report) that translates the model for a decision-maker (15/24).

### (b) Recommended outline + modelling toolkit, ranked by importance
**Toolkit (ranked):**
1. **Distortion-budgeted spanning arborescence for Problem 1** — model cumulative distortion multiplicatively (survival product ∏(1−d_e) ≥ 0.89) or additively with the 12% cap; solve as a constrained shortest-path-tree / Steiner-style heuristic: Dijkstra-like growth where each node's state is (cost, accumulated distortion), prune dominated labels; insert strong links exactly where the weak-only label set is infeasible (the Louvre min-cut logic: name the critical set). Cluster first (wildfire paper: k-means → per-cluster tree) if the flat heuristic is slow at n=1028.
2. **Per-edge loss conservation equations for Problem 2** (water paper's α_ij template): each city receives copies from ≤5 sources; define the fusion rule explicitly (e.g., error after cross-correction = product of independent survivals, or min-plus correction), prove the 3% threshold is met city-by-city, and switch regimes (single-source tree → multi-source fusion) the way the water papers switch LP → auction under scarcity.
3. **Perturbation-matrix robustness for Problem 3** (SDG paper): failures = node deletions; recompute feasibility/cost; report an anti-interference-style index (fraction of cities still ≤3%, added cost) averaged over many random 20-city draws, plus a centrality-ranked worst-case draw (Baltimore paper's betweenness screening) to show random ≠ worst.
4. **Recursive correction framing** (submersible Kalman): present cross-correction as a predict–update loop; report distortion as intervals under parameter jitter, with RMSE-style error accounting.
5. **Sensitivity done properly** (ShanghaiTech template): Morris screening over the distortion parameters (distance coefficient, out-degree coefficient, cap, strong-link distortion, receiver cost) + local derivatives with values; restrict headline claims to actionable parameters (Duke Maasai lesson).
6. **Sanity bounds**: MST lower bound on cost, weak-only infeasibility count, strong-link count vs threshold curve (11% → 3% sweep) — O papers always bracket their heuristic with a bound.

**Outline:** Summary Sheet → Contents → 1 Introduction (restatement + framework flowchart + network schematic) → 2 Assumptions *and justifications* → 3 Notation → 4 Problem 1 (model, algorithm with pseudocode, strong-link node list as a named table + map, bound comparison) → 5 Problem 2 (fusion model, regime switch, per-city threshold verification) → 6 Problem 3 (perturbation protocol, Monte Carlo results, emergency reconfiguration rule) → 7 Sensitivity & Error Analysis (Morris + local) → 8 Strengths & Weaknesses → 9 Conclusion → References → Appendix (full strong-link list / code).

### (c) Concrete figure plan for the 1028-city paper
1. **Fig. 1 (p. 1–2):** World map of the 1028 cities (point density) — map-first EDA (cybercrime paper pattern).
2. **Fig. 2:** Framework flowchart of the three-problem pipeline (14/24 convention).
3. **Fig. 3:** Schematic of weak vs strong link distortion accumulation along a path (principle diagram, wildfire pattern).
4. **Fig. 4:** Problem-1 solution map: tree edges, **strong links in red**, strong-link nodes marked — the Louvre red-min-cut figure, and the single most important figure in the paper.
5. **Fig. 5:** Cost vs distortion-threshold curve (11% → 3%) showing where strong links enter.
6. **Fig. 6:** Problem-2 multi-source assignment map (cities coloured by primary source; fusion cities marked).
7. **Fig. 7:** Before/after network pair for a Problem-3 failure draw (SDG perturbation pattern).
8. **Fig. 8:** Monte Carlo histogram of post-failure max distortion / added cost over ≥100 random draws.
9. **Fig. 9:** Morris μ–σ sensitivity plot + local-derivative bar chart.
10. **Table 1:** Notation. **Table 2:** Strong-link node list for Problem 1 (named deliverable). **Table 3:** Head-to-head comparison of model variants (weak-only / +strong / multi-source) — cost, max distortion, #strong links.
Caption style (from the library): captions tell the reader what to *do* with the figure ("Any attempt to improve the network should begin with the red edges"), and headline numbers are printed on solution maps themselves.

### (d) Summary-writing template (distilled)
> [Context sentence: what system, what tension.] For Problem 1, we [verb] … by [named model/algorithm], obtaining [headline number: total cost, #strong links, max distortion]. For Problem 2, we … [fusion rule in one clause], achieving [headline number] under the 3% threshold. For Problem 3, we … [perturbation protocol], and show [robustness number: e.g., in 100 random 20-city failures, ≥X% of cities remain within threshold at ≤Y% added cost]. [One honest limitation sentence.] Keywords: …
Sentence-level patterns: task verbs open each block ("First, to locate…"); every block = method + number; assumptions are justified in the body, never merely listed; recommendations are ranked; the closing sentence is a robustness claim (13/24).

### (e) Pitfalls / weak patterns to avoid
- Bare assumption lists (present even in one O paper — 2200084 — and visibly its weakest feature; the justified-assumption papers read stronger).
- Reporting raw network metrics without a benchmark or consequence (contrast 2002526's "9th of 20" benchmarking).
- Sensitivity as adjectives ("not sensitive") without numbers — every strong paper attaches a value or a bound.
- A Summary that describes methods but no results (off-pattern: 23/24 include headline numbers).
- Decorative figures (photos, hook quotes: 3/24 and 1/24 — idiosyncrasies, not conventions).
- Cross-mixing numbers between teams on the same problem (the three 2022 B papers report different survival horizons — in your own paper, never mix variants' numbers across sections).
- Claiming robustness from a single failure draw; O papers use scenario *sets*.
